# Portfolio voice

Use Clyde’s approved first-person voice across every portfolio page, including
Personal and Professional Home, About, Experience, projects, and writing.

- Sound like Clyde explaining his work to another person. Warm, straightforward,
  professional, and not stiff. Use short sentences and natural contractions.
- Use “I” for Clyde’s own work. Say “we” or name the team when the work was shared.
  Keep internship, contributor, and leadership roles accurate.
- Explain what the system does, how people use it, and what Clyde contributed.
  Avoid third-person biography and language that sounds like an audit report.
- “Basically,” “so,” “however,” and “actually” are natural connectors. Use them
  where they help, not as filler in every paragraph.
- Keep technical terms when they explain the work. Do not add unsupported
  achievements, metrics, experience, or ownership claims.
- Describe AI as part of Clyde’s workflow and learning, alongside understanding
  the code and testing it. Avoid generic AI slogans or defensive disclaimers.
- Keep selective blue/bold emphasis on useful skills and experience, not entire
  paragraphs. Professional mode is more compact, not a different personality.
- For future public-facing rewrites, show Clyde a representative draft before
  publishing. The September 3 first-person direction was reviewed and approved.

Approved example:

> I’m Clyde, a software engineer who works on web applications, interfaces, and
> business systems. Basically, I like understanding how a process works, then
> building something that makes it easier for people to use.
